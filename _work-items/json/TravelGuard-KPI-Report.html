1. Headline

48 days into the schema rollout, Travel Guard has taken the #1 brand-mention spot for the first time in this dataset — overtaking Seven Corners in the Jun 23 – Jul 6 window (521 vs 504). Every brand-level KPI now sits at or above its 90-day target.

Travel Guard is #1 (521 mentions vs Seven Corners' 504, having trailed by 74 at baseline). Brand coverage 33.9% is past the 32% 90-day target; own-domain citations 287 hold +55% over baseline; domain coverage 15% is past the 14% 90-day target; citation share steady at 2%.

The trajectory is not a straight line: citations dipped at Jun 23 (294 → 255) before recovering to 287, and several individual prompts regressed even as the headline numbers rose. Schema is plausibly contributing, but competitor decay (Seven Corners 537 → 504, World Nomads 426 → 360 across the two windows) and LLM index churn are clearly also at work — attribution stays shared, not clean.

7. What's attributable to schema vs. noise

The honest read: probably some, but not all.

  • Site-wide bundle (Organization + WebSite) has been live longest and has had the most time to propagate. Bing/ChatGPT search likely sees it; Google AI Overviews may not yet.
  • Per-page schemas (Plan, FAQ, Service, Article) were finalized in the repo only in the last ~6 days before the checkpoint — too recent for LLM indexes to have refreshed against them.
  • Competitor decay accounts for some of the relative gain — when Allianz and World Nomads lose ground, Travel Guard rises by default.
  • Content quality + index churn — the pre-existing-conditions +79% jump likely predates the schema being live, so most of that movement is content + LLM index updates, not yet schema.

What the Jun 23 + Jul 6 pulls tell us: the schema-first hypothesis held up where it's testable. The three prompts mapped to schema'd P0 pages (pre-existing, CFAR, baggage) each cleared their 90-day targets, and those same pages dominate the top-cited-URL list. That's the signal most consistent with schema working. Against it: citation share plateaued at 2% (not the 3% we hoped to see by now), the Jun 9 citation peak turned out to overstate the run-rate, and the biggest brand-rank gain leaned on competitor decay. Net read: schema is helping the pages it's been applied to; the brand-level #1 is real but partly a gift from a softening field.

What to watch at Day 90 (mid-Aug): whether citation share finally breaks above 2% (the make-or-break for the 3–4% target), whether the deploy of the built-but-undeployed baggage/assistance schema lifts those pages further, and whether the two regressing prompts (student safety, medical emergencies) stabilise or keep sliding.

How to update this report at each checkpoint

  1. Charts: in the <script> at the bottom of this file, append one object — { date:'2026-06-23', value: N, label:'Jun 23' } — to each metric's data array in SERIES. The charts redraw automatically; nothing else in the markup changes.
  2. Milestones: add deploy events to the MILESTONES array (date + label). They appear as numbered vertical lines once inside a chart's date range.
  3. Tables: update the "Current" / "Δ" cells in the §3 scorecard tables (and §5 per-prompt) to the new pull's values, and add a row to the checkpoint log above.
  4. Narrative: refresh the §1 headline callout and §7 attribution read with the new window's story.
  5. All numbers must come from the Otterly / GSC / Adobe Analytics pull — never estimated. Same content-fidelity rule as the JSON-LD work.
