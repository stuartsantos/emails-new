# 2026 Accomplishments

This year I rebranded and redesigned our email program from AIG Travel to Travel Guard / Zurich across nearly every brand and market we support. Across about 500 commits in the email repository, I touched more than 420 templates and built 168 new ones. That work covered Travel Guard US, Rest of World policy confirmations in 33 markets (many in multiple languages), Expedia in 19 markets, Qantas, Jetstar, United, Digital Direct, agent and admin templates. The project was originally scoped to need up to two dedicated offshore resources on top of my own work. Because I streamlined the workflow, I delivered it without them, and the company avoided that cost. Beyond the visual redesign, I made the whole library more consistent and reliable. I standardized dark-mode support, Outlook rendering, accessibility and campaign tracking across every template, cleaned up and retired outdated files, and built automated QA checks. Every template is now validated on each edit, which catches branding, accessibility and rendering problems before they reach customers.

A highlight was the direct-to-consumer (DTC) US program. By the end of May I had finished essentially all DTC email marketing for the rest of 2026: 45 emails in total. That included monthly Travel Tips through December, every holiday and seasonal send through New Year's 2027 (Thanksgiving, Travel Tuesday, Labor Day, winter season and others), the full post-purchase fulfillment series, the new Braze abandoned-cart and high-value journeys, and the Zurich Classic sponsorship emails. Because the work was done months ahead of schedule, Hannah could go on maternity leave with the full year's calendar already built, and the team needed no backfill or emergency coverage.

I also used AI to automate work that used to be manual and infrequent. Our robots.txt review used to be run by hand about once a month. I set it up as a scheduled AI workflow that now runs every week, so we catch crawl and indexing problems four times as often with no manual effort. I applied the same approach to our SEO and site-health reporting, with automated weekly performance reports (Lighthouse and real-user Core Web Vitals) and weekly KPI checkpoints that publish without anyone having to start them.

Finally, I designed and began building an in-house AI compliance reviewer as an alternative to licensing HAAST, which would have cost the company about $350K a year. The tool reviews Word drafts and live travelguard.com pages against Legal's compliance rules and our brand voice, and it keeps an audit trail of every decision. It also learns from past compliance outcomes. In about three weeks I took it from a project plan to a working application with the rules import, the review engine, a user interface and a clickable demo. Taken together, this year's work goes beyond delivering what was asked. It cut costs, removed delivery risk and freed up team capacity through automation.
